# v0.4 Time Axis

Status: companion strategy for the `v0.4.0` proof-engine pivot. Source issue:
#1050. Pair with [`v0.4-proof-engine.md`](./v0.4-proof-engine.md), which
defines the release gate and receipt method.

## Purpose

The v0.4 proof-engine strategy claims current white space: no competitor is
running controlled experiments on how developers work with coding agents. This
document asks a longer question: if competitors notice the same opening, what
still compounds six to twelve months from now?

The answer is the time axis. First-mover positioning decays. Calendar-time
assets compound.

## Core Claim

The durable moat is not "we saw the proof-engine niche first." It is three
assets that become harder to copy with every month that passes:

1. **The longitudinal local-history corpus.** A user cannot buy back a year of
   their own agent history after deciding they need it.
2. **The receipt and revalidation archive.** Proofs tied to model versions can
   be re-run, re-stamped, or revoked as providers change.
3. **Reputation for honest methodology.** Pre-registration, null receipts, and
   outside review compound slowly and are hard to fake under growth pressure.

The strategic instruction is: start the clock on assets that cannot be bought
later.

## Six-Month View

By about December 2026, assume more frontier model releases and continued
first-party absorption of descriptive analytics. Every model release invalidates
static workflow advice, but it also creates a reason to re-run this product's
receipt corpus. That turns provider churn from a threat into a recurring proof
event.

The likely pressure point is not necessarily a public price increase. It may be
tighter caps, fair-use enforcement, tier restructuring, or less generous access.
As the gap between subscription cost and API-equivalent consumption narrows,
efficiency shifts from curiosity to constraint. The first credible proof receipt
needs to exist before that pressure arrives, not after.

Seed-funded competitors such as Backplanes have a different incentive. They
need breadth, logos, integrations, and category vocabulary. Their likely move is
more reporting, more personas, and more organization views. The counter-position
is not to out-report them. It is to publish proof and force the conversation
onto a question reports cannot answer: "Can you prove the workflow change caused
the lift?"

Immediate implication: the first v0.4 proof receipt should include model-version
scope and a revalidation path. The first provider release after the receipt
becomes the second marketing event: the corpus was re-run, and the receipt was
kept, changed, or revoked.

## Twelve-Month View

Two pricing futures both preserve the proof-engine bet.

If inference prices normalize upward or subscription subsidies shrink,
efficiency engineering becomes budgeted work. Buyers need evidence, not another
dashboard. Receipts become procurement and rollout artifacts.

If inference prices collapse, cost proof becomes less important, but proof does
not. The valuable question moves from "which workflow is cheaper?" to "which
workflow, model, or harness produces better engineering outcomes?" The same
apparatus answers it: matched pairs, objective gates, jailed worktrees, and
pre-registered analysis.

Dollars are the wedge, not the destination. Receipt schemas should keep quality,
latency, reliability, and cost as co-equal dimensions.

## Harness Fragmentation

The public voice can stay Claude-native for v0.4: the independent proof engine
built on your own Claude history. The schema should not be trapped there.

Keep the receipt format, fixture-corpus schema, and proof ledger harness-agnostic
now, even while #844 stays parked as product scope. That makes Claude-native
positioning a go-to-market choice rather than an architectural commitment.

## Governance Tailwinds

Cloud aggregation of agent sessions creates a security and compliance surface:
files touched, commands run, MCP access, credentials nearby, and policy
exceptions. Local-first storage and reproducible proof receipts are not only
product differentiators; they can become audit artifacts.

Do not overbuild for regulation in v0.4. Preserve the option: keep proofs
reproducible, keep egress local-first, and keep receipt claims explicit about
what they do and do not prove.

## Bootstrap Asymmetry

VC competitors convert capital into speed. This project must convert time into
assets capital cannot instantly reproduce.

| Asset | Why it compounds | Product implication |
|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal corpus | Past personal agent history cannot be backfilled after the fact. | Prioritize durable local capture and retention over dashboard parity. |
| Receipt archive | Each model generation creates a new proof-decay event. | Treat revalidation as core product behavior, not metadata. |
| Methodological reputation | Honest nulls and outside review accrue credibility slowly. | Publish nulls, pre-register batches, and make review references first-class. |

The survival strategy is not a land grab. Stay default-alive, keep burn low,
let funded players educate the market, and own the credible version of the
category when efficiency and governance pressure rise.

## Priority Changes

This document does not replace the v0.4 proof-engine plan. It re-weights it.

1. The proof receipt gate (#995) remains the release strategy.
2. Model-version scope and receipt revalidation move from "nice receipt field"
   to long-term moat behavior.
3. Corpus accumulation and retention outrank parity features unless a parity
   feature directly increases proof quality or corpus durability.
4. Harness-agnostic schemas should be kept cheap now; broad product
   generalization can wait.
5. Competitive monitoring should focus less on new dashboards and more on any
   competitor adopting pre-registration, revalidation, or causal workflow
   claims.

## Non-Goals

- This is not a commitment to build multi-tenant infrastructure in v0.4.
- This is not a prediction that prices must rise; the strategy works if prices
  rise or fall.
- This is not a replacement for the competitive-analysis notes. It is the
  time-horizon strategy that those notes imply.
