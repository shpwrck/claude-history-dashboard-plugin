# v0.4 Proof — External review of the corrected-corpus NULL (#1078, run #2083)

**Verdict: ACCEPT WITH REQUIRED CAVEATS.** The corrected-corpus run is a
statistically correct, honestly-reported NULL, and I reproduced every number in
its receipt from the published per-pair costs (paired-median delta −3.30% vs the
receipt's −3.33%, W−=44, Wilcoxon p=0.362, bootstrap CI [−0.083, +0.086], all
seed=1) — the §5 decision rule in `proof-stats.ts` was applied correctly and the
NULL classification is unambiguous (the delta is ~5× short of the −15% MDE, the
CI straddles zero, and p≫0.05). The Amendment 2 fixture repair did **not**
manufacture the verdict: both runs land NULL and the repair, if anything, moved
the median slightly *more* negative. **However**, the experiment as built is
structurally underpowered to detect its own treatment by roughly an order of
magnitude: the "stable files" are 224–499 bytes, so the maximum theoretical
cross-session re-read saving the mechanism can produce is ≲1% of chain cost
against a 15% MDE. This is a *clean null about a near-zero-magnitude mechanism on
a tiny-file corpus*, not evidence that "referencing stable files doesn't save
tokens" in general. The receipt may be persisted only if it carries the caveats
in §5 below, which bound the claim to what the apparatus can actually support.

## 2. Adversarial threats to validity

Threats are tagged by what they endanger: the NULL's **direction** (could the
true effect actually be a win the apparatus hid?) vs its **interpretation** (the
NULL is real but means less/other than a casual reader assumes). For a NULL, the
dangerous threats are direction threats — anything that would *suppress* a real
effect. Most threats below are interpretation threats, which is the benign
direction for a null, with two exceptions noted.

### T1 — Construct validity / effect-size ceiling (SEVERE; interpretation, and it neuters the test)
This is the dominant finding. The treatment removes *re-paid tokens for
re-reading a stable file across cold sessions*. The actual stable files are
tiny:

| stable file | bytes | ~tokens |
|---|---|---|
| `units.mjs` | 359 | ~90 |
| `rules.mjs` | 224 | ~60 |
| `event-bus.mjs` | 499 | ~125 |
| `schema.mjs` | 279 | ~70 |

A chain re-reads such a file at most once per session × 8 sessions. The
theoretical maximum saving:

- @ cache-read price (~$0.30/Mtok): ~$0.0002/chain → **0.02%** of a ~$1 chain
- @ normal-input price (~$1.5/Mtok): ~$0.001/chain → **0.1%**
- @ full Opus input price (~$15/Mtok, most generous): ~$0.011/chain → **~1.1%**

Against a **15% MDE**, the mechanism's own ceiling is ~1% under the most generous
pricing assumption. **The experiment is underpowered to detect its treatment by
~15×, by construction.** Whatever the true effect, it is an order of magnitude
below the threshold the batch was declared "powered" to detect (§5 of the
pre-registration). The −3.3% observed delta is therefore not "the effect" — it is
run noise (see T3) that is itself larger than the entire mechanism. This does not
threaten the NULL's direction (a sub-1% effect *is* indistinguishable from null
at this MDE), but it sharply limits interpretation: this is a null about a
near-zero-magnitude mechanism on a tiny-file corpus, not a refutation of
stable-file referencing in general.

Note this sits in tension with Amendment 1 §2 constraint 4 ("realistic stable
files ... never bloated to inflate per-read tokens"). The constraint correctly
forbids *inflating* file size to manufacture an effect — but the corpus has
swung to the opposite extreme where the files are so small the effect is
unmeasurable. Both directions defeat a powered test; the corpus sits at the
unmeasurable end.

### T2 — The unpriced premise (SEVERE; interpretation, upstream of the whole experiment)
The receipt records `observedUsdPerMo = 0`: the #890 detector does **not** fire
on real `~/.claude` history. The finding-on-the-table that the generic re-read
rule it replaces fires at only ~$33/mo compounds this. So even a *proven* effect
would project to ≈$0/mo reclaim (the projection formula = observed $/mo ×
%reduction, and the receipt's `projection.reclaimUsdPerMo` is 0). The experiment
is proving the efficacy of a recommendation whose real-world incidence is ~zero.
This doesn't threaten the NULL's validity; it questions whether the treatment was
worth a 576-session, $80 batch at all. For the receipt's honesty it must be
stated plainly: a NULL here is over-determined — the mechanism is tiny (T1) *and*
the pattern it targets is absent from real history (T2).

### T3 — Run-to-run noise dominates the signal (SEVERE corroborating evidence; supports the NULL)
I compared the 9 *unrepaired* pairs across the broken run
(`proof-full-run-2083.log`) and the corrected run. Their per-pair deltas are
**not stable**: the 9-pair median delta is +0.040 in the broken run and −0.030 in
the corrected run — a swing larger than the entire claimed effect, on identical
fixtures. Individual pairs move by $0.05–$0.30 between runs. With k=3 and ~$1
chains, between-run variance swamps a sub-1% mechanism. This is the mechanical
reason the CI is so wide ([−8%, +9%]) and is strong corroboration that the NULL
is a noise-floor null, not a measured-small-effect null. (It also means the
"dose-response" curve, see T8, is largely noise.)

### T4 — Quality-hold is coarse-grained (MODERATE; could mask a direction-relevant quality drop)
The quality hold is computed at **arm granularity**, not run granularity:
`armSuccess = successes.length > 0` (`proof-batch.mjs:535`), then the rate is the
fraction of pairs whose arm had **≥1 of 3** successful runs
(`proof-batch.mjs:604`). So an arm that succeeds 1/3 and an arm that succeeds 3/3
both count as a full "success" for the quality hold. The corrected run reports
100%/100%, but this metric would not distinguish "treatment quietly halved the
success *rate* but still passed once per pair." For a cost-win claim this would
be a real gap (you could buy cost savings with reliability). Here it is only a
moderate threat because (a) the verdict is NULL on cost anyway, so there's no win
to be undermined, and (b) the per-run `ok N/3` columns in the log show 3/3 across
all pairs in the corrected run. Recommend the receipt note this metric measures
per-pair *any-success*, not per-run success rate.

### T5 — Treatment's own CLAUDE.md token cost (LOW; biases toward the NULL, safe direction)
The treatment appends the `## Stable reference files` block to a CLAUDE.md
present in every session (`proof-batch.mjs:321`). That injected text costs input
tokens every session that control does not pay. This *works against* H1 (it adds
cost to the treatment arm), so it can only shrink or reverse a measured saving —
the safe direction for a null. It does, however, partly explain the 4/12 pairs
where treatment cost *more*. Not a threat to the NULL.

### T6 — Fixtures authored by LLM subagents (MODERATE; interpretation)
The fixtures and chains were authored by the same family of agents whose
behavior is under test, and the anti-gaming constraints (Amendment 1 §2) are
self-attested, not independently audited. The self-correcting argument (a control
agent that guesses symbols without reading either fails Gate 0 or also saves
tokens — both shrink the delta, biasing against H1) is sound *as far as it goes*
and points in the safe direction. But it does not address T1: well-intentioned
"realistic, un-bloated" authoring produced files too small to measure. The threat
is to interpretation (is this corpus representative of the real waste pattern?),
and given T2 the honest answer is "no — the real pattern barely exists."

### T7 — Adherence measure is not in this run's log + has limits (MODERATE; interpretation of the "real-mechanism null" claim)
The runner prints an adherence column (`rd`, median stable-file tool touches per
chain — `proof-batch.mjs:548`), but **the corrected-run log I was given does not
contain it** (no `rd`/`adherence` lines in `proof-rerun-2083-corrected.log`). The
"treatment ~0 vs control ~8" adherence finding is attributed to an **n=4
calibration**, not to the 12/12 run under review. So the central claim that "the
null is a real-mechanism null, not non-adoption" rests on a 4-sample side
measurement, not on the run that produced the verdict. Further, `countStableReads`
(`proof-batch.mjs:187`) counts *tool invocations whose input JSON contains the
stable path string* — it counts citations and `Read`/`Grep`/`Bash` mentions
alike, and it cannot distinguish a full file read from a one-line grep. It is a
coarse proxy for "bytes re-paid," which is the quantity that actually drives cost.
Combined with T1, even perfect adherence (treatment reads the file 0 times)
saves ≲1%, so adherence is somewhat moot — but the receipt should not lean on the
"honored, real-mechanism null" framing without (a) the per-run adherence numbers
from *this* batch and (b) acknowledging the proxy's coarseness. The one
calibration pair (validator) that did *not* honor the recommendation and was most
expensive is a single anecdote, not evidence.

### T8 — Dose-response is presented as onset but is non-monotone noise (LOW; interpretation)
The dose-response curve is correctly fenced as SUPPORTING and barred from setting
the verdict (Amendment 1 §4 forecloses M-shopping — a genuine p-hacking guard,
good). But the curve itself is **not monotone**: s2 is *positive* (+$0.005) and
s6 is less negative than s5, before s7/s8 drift down. A clean cross-session onset
would be monotone in magnitude; this wanders, consistent with T3 noise rather
than an accumulating effect. Risk: a reader could over-read the s7→s8 drift as
"the effect kicks in late." The receipt should not narrate the dose curve as
evidence of onset.

### T9 — n=12 sufficiency / power (MODERATE; structural)
N=12 with k=3 is the pre-registered minimum and was honored. But N was fixed from
a workflow rationale ("a feature over a work-week," Amendment 1 §1.1),
**explicitly not tuned to the MDE.** Given T1 (true effect ≲1%) and T3 (per-pair
SD on the order of $0.10 on ~$1 chains), the achieved power to detect even a real
1% effect at α=0.05 is negligible — you would need hundreds of pairs, or far
larger files, or far longer chains. This is a power threat, but for a NULL it is
not fatal: the honest reading is "underpowered for any sub-MDE effect," which the
caveats already require. It would be fatal only if someone tried to read this
NULL as positive evidence of *no* effect (absence of evidence ≠ evidence of
absence).

### T10 — p-hacking / forking-paths surface across two runs + the repair (MODERATE; mostly defused — see §3)
Two runs exist (broken, corrected) and a fixture repair sits between them. That
is a forking-paths surface. I examined it directly (§3) and find it did not bend
the verdict, but it is not zero-risk and the receipt must disclose both runs (it
already links them).

### T11 — Cost source heterogeneity (LOW; interpretation)
`costFromWorkerJson` (`proof-batch.mjs:141`) prefers the worker's
`total_cost_usd` and falls back to token-priced estimation. If some sessions
reported `total_cost_usd` and others fell back to estimation, the per-chain cost
mixes two rulers. The matched-pairs design cancels this if both arms use the same
source per pair, which is likely but not verified in the log. Low impact on a
NULL.

## 3. Integrity of the Amendment 2 corpus repair

The repair fixed three fixtures that failed Gate 0 in **both** arms in the broken
run (`stable-api-build-add-export`, `config-backed-flag-rename-diff`,
`stable-api-codec-roundtrip` — `proof-full-run-2083.log:6,12,14`, each `ok 0`).
A both-arms-failing pair is a broken instrument: its paired delta is ≈0 noise and
it pollutes the success-conditional quality hold. I verified the repair could not
have manufactured the verdict, three ways:

1. **Selection was direction-blind.** The three repaired pairs are exactly the
   three with 0/3 success in *both* arms — selected on failure-in-both-arms, a
   property orthogonal to the sign of the cost delta. Two of the three even had
   *negative* (cost-reducing, pro-H1) deltas in the broken run (−0.10, −0.22), so
   "repair the ones that didn't show the effect" is not what happened.
2. **The verdict was already NULL on the other nine.** The 9 unrepaired pairs
   produced a NULL in the broken run too. Replacing three ≈0-signal pairs with
   working ones cannot turn a NULL into a PROVEN unless the new pairs carried a
   huge pro-treatment effect — they did not (corrected repaired deltas: −0.06,
   −0.10, **+0.12**; the codec pair flipped to *cost more*).
3. **Both runs land NULL, far from MDE.** Broken: −1.67%. Corrected: −3.33%.
   Neither is within shouting distance of −15%. The repair moved the headline
   median ~1.7 points more negative — and Amendment 2 §0 pre-committed that a flip
   to PROVEN off this repair "would itself be a red flag to investigate, not a win
   to claim." No flip occurred. **The repair is legitimate instrument repair, not
   result-shopping.**

One honest wrinkle (T10): the corrected run re-ran **all 12** pairs, not just the
3 repaired, so run-to-run noise on the 9 unchanged pairs (their 9-pair median
swung +0.040 → −0.030, §T3) flowed into the new headline. A stricter design would
have held the 9 fixed and only re-run the 3. This does not threaten the verdict
class (NULL both ways) but means the −3.33% vs −1.67% difference is mostly noise,
not "the repair revealed a slightly larger effect." The receipt should not claim
the corrected number is a more accurate effect estimate — only that it is a
cleaner *instrument* (12 measuring pairs vs 9).

Process integrity is otherwise good: amendments are by commit predating the run,
thresholds are frozen and were not touched, N stayed 12, no pair was dropped, and
the single-primary-test rule (Amendment 1 §4) forecloses M-shopping.

## 4. What the result legitimately supports vs. does not

**Legitimately supports:**
- On *this corpus* (12 matched chains, M=8, tiny stable files, Opus 4.8),
  injecting the #890 stable-reference CLAUDE.md produces **no detectable
  per-chain cost reduction at the pre-registered 15% MDE**; the point estimate is
  −3.3% with a CI spanning roughly ±8%.
- **No quality degradation** was observed (100%/100% at arm granularity; 3/3 per
  run in the log).
- The decision apparatus is **sound and reproducible** — a skeptic re-running the
  frozen bundle and seed gets these numbers.
- The repair was a clean instrument fix; the NULL is robust to it.

**Does NOT support:**
- It does **not** show that "referencing stable files instead of re-reading them
  saves nothing" as a general claim. The mechanism's own ceiling on this corpus
  is ≲1% (T1), so the test could not have detected a real effect of the size the
  mechanism can produce. This is a null *about an unmeasurably small mechanism*,
  not a refutation.
- It does **not** establish the treatment is useless in the wild — and separately
  (T2) the #890 pattern barely occurs in real history, so the question is largely
  academic for now.
- It does **not** support any onset/dose narrative (T8, non-monotone noise).
- It is **not** "evidence of no effect" in the confirmatory sense (T9, negligible
  power for sub-MDE effects).

## 5. Verdict and recommendation

**ACCEPT WITH REQUIRED CAVEATS.** The NULL is correctly computed, honestly
classified, and robust to the repair; it is credible to persist as a receipt. But
it must not be read as a clean refutation of the treatment, and the receipt must
carry the construct-validity and premise limits so a downstream consumer of
`/api/recommendations.json` (or a future revalidation) does not over-claim. The
following caveat text must be attached to the receipt (e.g. appended to
`rollout`, or via `--qualification` so it rides into the persisted record):

> **External-review qualification (#1078, run #2083).** This NULL is *correct but
> low-information*. (1) **Effect-size ceiling:** the corpus's stable files are
> 224–499 bytes, so the cross-session re-read the treatment removes is worth
> ≲1% of chain cost even under full Opus input pricing — against a 15% MDE the
> batch is ~15× underpowered to detect its own mechanism *by construction*. Read
> this as "no effect *of detectable size on this tiny-file corpus*," NOT as a
> refutation of stable-file referencing in general. (2) **Premise:** the #890
> detector does not fire on real history (observedUsdPerMo = 0), so projected
> reclaim is ≈$0/mo regardless of verdict; the pattern's real-world incidence is
> near-zero. (3) **Noise floor:** between-run per-pair deltas swing more than the
> claimed effect (the 9 unrepaired pairs' median moved +4.0% → −3.0% across runs
> on identical fixtures), so −3.3% is noise, not a measured small effect; the CI
> [−8%, +9%] reflects this. (4) **Adherence** that "treatment honored the
> reference" comes from a 4-sample calibration, not this 12/12 run, and uses a
> coarse path-mention tool-invocation proxy; it should not be cited as proof of a
> "real-mechanism null" without per-run adherence from this batch. (5) The
> dose-response curve is non-monotone noise and sets nothing. (6) Quality-hold is
> measured at per-pair any-success granularity, not per-run success rate.
> **Actionable read:** before any future revalidation, fix the apparatus, not the
> stat — enlarge the stable files to a realistic module size (or lengthen the
> chains) so the mechanism's ceiling exceeds the MDE, OR retarget the proof to a
> waste pattern that actually fires on real history. As built, this question is
> not answerable at the powered MDE; the receipt records that honestly.

Rationale for ACCEPT-WITH-CAVEATS over REJECT: the apparatus, statistics, and
process integrity are genuinely sound — I could not break the math or find that
the repair bent the verdict, and a credible scientific null (per Amendment 1 §6:
N≥12 reached, §5 rule returned NULL) is a real artifact worth keeping on record.
The failure here is one of *experimental design power*, not of execution or
honesty, and the design limitation is disclosable. A REJECT would be warranted
only if the verdict were unreproducible, the repair had manufactured it, or the
apparatus could not produce *any* trustworthy verdict — none of which holds. The
caveats convert a potentially misleading "we proved it doesn't help" into the
accurate "we couldn't measure it at this scale, and the target barely exists,"
which is the honest and useful artifact.
