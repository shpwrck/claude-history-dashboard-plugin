## The leading question

> **Can this product PROVE — not suggest — that a specific change in how you
> work with your agent leaves you measurably better off?**

This is the operationalized form of the question that opened the release:
*"Is there a business here?"* Competitive research (deposited on #976) showed
the business exists if and only if the answer is yes:

- The **descriptive layer is commoditized.** Anthropic shipped per-user cost
  APIs and a console dashboard inside eight months (Claude Code Analytics API
  Sep 2025, Enterprise Analytics API May 2026) and officially recommends five
  third-party FinOps partners on top. The OSS floor is six-plus free trackers.
  Competing there means racing a free first-party API downhill.
- The **prescriptive layer has a structural moat.** A per-token vendor will
  ship cost *transparency* but not aggressive "your workflow burns 3x the
  tokens it should" *coaching* — the AWS-vs-FinOps pattern. Session-level
  forensics on local `~/.claude` data is a layer the vendor cannot centralize
  without a privacy reversal.
- **Causal workflow experimentation is white space.** No product anywhere runs
  controlled experiments on how developers work with coding agents. The
  nearest occupant (Faros AI "Experiments") is post-hoc, aggregate, and
  leadership-facing — structurally a different product.

So v0.4 stops competing on accounting and bets the release on the one thing
only this product can do: **close the loop from recommendation to proof.**

v0.3 is untouched by this pivot. Its efficiency accounting stops being the
headline and becomes the **ruler** — the measurement substrate every proof is
priced against.

## What ships: one proof receipt, or an honest verdict

The release gate is binary (epic #995): **one externally-reviewed proof
receipt from a pre-registered workflow experiment** — or an explicit, honest
negative.

### The experiment

| Element | Decision |
|---|---|
| **Treatment** | ONE high-evidence recommendation the engine already emits, **injected vs withheld** (#582 recs axis, #583 eligibility screen, #579 gated efficacy detector — ADR 0005 Tier 2). The receipt thereby proves *the engine's own prescriptions are causal* — the business claim itself. Front-runner per evidence-strength ranking: the #890 repo-map context-waste family; the final pick happens at pre-registration. |
| **Corpus** | *History prices, fixtures prove.* Real `~/.claude` history supplies the observed waste pattern and its real-dollar magnitude (v0.3 accounting). A controlled fixture corpus with objective test gates (shared substrate from #975) carries the causal proof as matched pairs — same task, control vs injected, jailed worktrees. H1 history-replay confirmation is an optional upgrade, **not** the gate. |
| **Verdict source** | Objective gates (build/test/diff, cost, latency) produce the verdict; the LLM judge is demoted to tiebreaker (#598). This is the direct answer to the "replay was sterile" postmortem on #594 — aesthetic judging and corpus monoculture are designed out, not patched over. |

### The credibility bar

Methods for a methods-literate skeptic; packaging for an engineering manager;
gate event external:

1. Hypothesis and analysis plan **pre-registered in-repo before the batch
   runs** (treatment rec, fixture set, N, decision rule, and the **minimum
   detectable effect** the batch is powered for — effects below it ship as
   "indistinguishable from null", per the epistemic boundaries below).
2. Effect size with uncertainty and the matched-pair count published.
3. A reproducible fixture bundle a skeptic can re-run.
4. **At least one real external review** — publication or a named hostile
   reviewer.
5. One-page presentation, dollars first, methods as appendix — a manager
   reading one developer's receipt should see the org story
   ("solo proof, team framing").

### The bust taxonomy (honest by construction)

- **Scientific null** — the pre-registered batch yields no decisive effect:
  ships as a **null receipt**, published with the same rigor. A credible null
  still answers the leading question.
- **Engineering failure** — the pre-registered batch cannot run: reported
  explicitly as *"not yet provable,"* never silently. The milestone may not
  end in a shrug.

## The proof receipt

Extends the existing adoption receipt (writer landed in d31f114) with the
efficacy half ADR 0005 deferred:

- experiment ref + **pre-registration ref** (the commit that predates the batch)
- observed: waste pattern, detector ref, **$X/mo from real history** (v0.3 accounting)
- experiment: fixture-set ref, design (matched pairs, injected vs withheld), N, objective gates
- result: effect size + uncertainty, per-dimension deltas, verdict (proven / null / refuted)
- projection: $Y/mo reclaim with stated assumptions (the fixtures→history bridge is an extrapolation and says so)
- rollout: the prescription, written team-legibly
- external-review ref
- **model-version scope + revalidation status**: every effect is conditional on
  the model version the batch ran against; the receipt names that version and
  carries a freshness state (current / stale / revoked) maintained by re-running
  the fixture corpus when the model changes (see *Proof decay* below)

## Epistemic boundaries — what a receipt can and cannot claim

Addendum 2026-06-10 (#1043), answering the maintainer's stress-test: *"How much
can we really prove on top of non-deterministic models?"* The short answer:
distributional causal claims with quantified uncertainty — which is exactly the
claim shape the receipt above already encodes. This section pins the boundary
so no receipt, landing copy, or one-pager ever claims past it.

### Non-determinism is the normal condition of proof, not a blocker

Every field that runs controlled experiments (medicine, agriculture, web A/B)
proves causal effects on systems noisier than an LLM. None proves "this run
will be better"; all prove "treatment shifts the outcome distribution by E,
CI [a,b], N=k." The honest question is never "can we prove over noise" but
"is the effect large enough, and N affordable, for the noise we have."

### Two claim classes

- **Class A — accounting effects.** Deterministic arithmetic, no experiment
  needed: bytes removed from a cached prefix, a posted Batch API discount,
  rate-card deltas between models. Provable by construction with the v0.3
  ruler alone.
- **Class B — behavioral effects.** Whether injecting a recommendation changes
  the agent's trajectory distribution (tokens-to-green, cost, latency,
  quality). Fully exposed to model stochasticity; this is what the experiment
  apparatus exists for.

**Lever-choice rule:** prefer treatments that are mostly Class A with a
Class B rider — the token reduction is near-mechanical and the experiment only
has to show quality holds. The #890 repo-map front-runner fits this shape;
that is part of why it leads the evidence-strength ranking.

### Design consequences of the noise we actually have

- **Variance is heavy-tailed** (one runaway loop blows a mean), so verdicts
  use medians and paired non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon signed-rank / sign
  test) or bootstrap CIs — never raw means. A tail claim ("fewer blowup runs")
  can be both more provable and more valuable than a mean claim.
- **Pairing carries the proof.** Between-task variance dwarfs between-run
  variance; the matched-pair design (same fixture, control vs injected) cancels
  the dominant noise source. Each arm runs **k >= 2–3 times per fixture** so
  within-pair variance is estimated, not assumed.
- **Power is pre-registered.** The analysis plan states the minimum detectable
  effect the batch is powered for. Small effects under this variance need an N
  whose token bill defeats the purpose; below-MDE results ship as
  "indistinguishable from null" — a scientific null, not a shrug.
- **Temperature 0 would not rescue determinism** (MoE routing, batched
  inference, floating point, plus harness-level noise from tool results and
  timing). Averaging over runs is the only path; the design assumes it.

### Hard limits — never claimed on a receipt

1. **Generalization beyond the fixture corpus.** The fixtures→history bridge
   stays labeled an extrapolation (already required by the receipt format).
2. **Model-version stability.** Every proof is conditional on the model the
   batch ran against. A receipt proven on one model is a *hypothesis* about
   the next.
3. **Long-horizon compounding** ("recursively better"). Too many confounds
   across weeks of real work; this is the #726 unfalsifiable trap, designed
   out, not attempted.
4. **Per-run guarantees.** Distributions only — never "this session will be
   cheaper."

### Proof decay is a product property, not a bug

Limit 2 looks like a weakness; it is the recurring-value engine. Receipts
carry a model-version scope, and when the model version changes the engine
re-runs the fixture corpus and **re-stamps or revokes** the receipt. A one-off
study goes stale silently; this product's receipts age honestly and renew
mechanically. No narrative or report-style competitor can match "our proofs
expire and we re-validate them" without rebuilding their product around a
reproducible corpus.

### Positioning note: the noise is the moat

If models were deterministic, "proof" would be `diff run-a run-b` — trivially
commoditized within a sprint of any competitor deciding to ship it. Proving
anything over this much variance requires the apparatus this product already
has (jailed reproducible worktrees, matched pairs, objective gates,
pre-registration, the ledger) — a methodology, not a feature. And the
epistemic honesty itself differentiates: against competitors shipping
unreplicated model-narrated summaries, the receipt that states what it
*cannot* conclude is the one a methods-literate skeptic believes.

## Decision record (2026-06-09 grill, seven forks)

1. **Gate = workflow proof or bust.** Not the #975 routing proof — that is
   demoted to secondary. A null outcome still answers the leading question.
2. **Treatment = the engine's own recommendation**, injected vs withheld.
3. **Corpus = history prices, fixtures prove.**
4. **Credibility = pre-registration + objective gates + external review;
   null ships as a null receipt.**
5. **#975 re-scoped, substrate shared:** its eval-result schema and curated
   fixture-corpus units are critical path for THE proof (one schema, one
   corpus, two consumers); gap mining, clustering, batch generation, and the
   Model Evals workbench remain in-milestone as secondary — a second receipt
   if the substrate holds.
6. **SPA = real receipt or nothing.** The marketing surface shows only
   externally-validated evidence. No SAMPLE proof receipts, ever. Until the
   gate passes, landing copy may describe the method, not claim results.
7. **#844 parked; Claude-native voice leads.** Positioning is one sentence —
   *the independent proof engine built on your own Claude history* (#996).
   Generalize from strength after the first proof. Vocabulary rule: never
   "A/B testing AI agents" (that names shadow-testing a shipped agent
   feature); say *experiments on developers using the agent*.

## Relationship to v0.3

legible"). Every dollar figure on a proof receipt is computed by the v0.3
accounting; the proof engine is the first consumer that turns that ledger
from a report into evidence. Nothing in v0.3 is reverted, re-themed, or
blocked by this pivot.
